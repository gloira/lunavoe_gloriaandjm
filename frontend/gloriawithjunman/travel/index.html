Before Bali

A few practical things worth sorting out before you fly, so the weekend feels simple from the very start.

Before you go

Visa & entry

Bali is straightforward for most travellers, though entry rules vary slightly depending on where you’re coming from.

  • China: Visa on Arrival (VOA / e-VOA), usually 30 days and extendable once.
  • Singapore: Visa-free entry for short leisure stays.
  • Malaysia: Visa-free entry for short leisure stays.
  • Make sure your passport has at least 6 months validity from arrival.
Good to know

Payments & currency

Hotels and most restaurants are easy, though Bali still feels more relaxed if you carry a little cash for the smaller moments.

  • Visa and Mastercard are the most widely accepted cards.
  • Some smaller shops, warungs, and casual local spots may still prefer cash.
  • It’s useful to keep some Indonesian Rupiah (IDR) with you, especially for small purchases.

Once you land

Bali moves at its own pace. A little extra time — and the right apps — make everything much easier.

Getting around

Transport & ride apps

The easiest way to get around Bali is by ride-hailing app or a private driver, depending on how many stops you have planned for the day.

Grab Easy for airport transfers, point-to-point rides, and general use around the island.
Gojek Also widely used in Bali for rides, food delivery, and quick everyday errands.
Private driver Best if you’re planning a full day out with several stops or exploring further afield.
Traffic in Bali can be much slower than it looks on a map, especially in the afternoon and around sunset — so it’s always worth leaving a little earlier than you think.

If you keep exploring

Bali is wonderfully different from one pocket of the island to the next. Here’s the version we’d suggest if you want to extend the weekend a little.

Explore Bali

A simple guide to Bali’s main areas

If you’re deciding where to spend an extra day or two, think of Bali in moods: cliffs and sunsets in the south, restaurants and beach clubs further west, cafés and social energy around Canggu, and rice terraces inland toward Ubud.

Uluwatu Best for cliffside views, beach afternoons, and some of Bali’s most memorable sunsets.
Seminyak Ideal for restaurants, boutique shopping, and a more polished beach-club style of day.
Canggu A little livelier — good for cafés, relaxed dinners, and an easygoing social atmosphere.
Ubud If you would like a gentler contrast to the coast: rice terraces, temples, and a slower inland rhythm.
Kintamani Known for mountain air, volcano views, and a very different side of Bali from the coast.
Lovina Further north, with black-sand beaches, early dolphin tours, and a quieter pace.
Nusa Penida Ideal for dramatic coastal views, bright water, and a full-day island escape.
